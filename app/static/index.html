Stage 01 · Ingestion

Source documents

Every file is cut into blocks that keep their page number, then stored as spans. Nothing downstream ever cites a file — it cites a span. That is what turns “check this claim” into a database query instead of a re-reading exercise.

Upload & parse

Cheapest correct parser first: pypdf_text (real page numbers) → anydoc (Rust, office formats) → vision_llm_ocr (scanned pages). None downloads a model.

Stage 02 · Extraction

Curriculum structure

A model proposes the topics, objectives and prerequisites hidden in the syllabus prose. Everything it proposes is stamped machine-extracted and scored below certainty — an inferred prerequisite must never wear the same authority as one the syllabus states outright.

Nodes are embedded straight afterwards; the mapper's shortlist can only see objectives that carry a vector.

Stage 03 · Extraction

Questions & marks

A question and its mark scheme are pulled out as one linked entity, both halves carrying page attribution. The unmatched counts are reported rather than buried — a paper whose questions mostly fail to match its scheme is telling you the numbering conventions differ.

Stage 04 · Analysis

Objective mapping

Four signals — embedding similarity, lexical overlap, syllabus terminology and a model's judgement — combine into weighted, multi-label mappings. One question can assess several objectives at once, and forcing it into a single label would misrepresent the paper.

Run the ensemble

The confidence floor is a teacher's knob: raise it for precision, lower it for recall.

Stage 05 · Feedback

Teacher corrections

A correction is written to the record verbatim and then applied: confidence pinned to certain, method rewritten to human-corrected. This is the one signal the ensemble never argues with, and the log of it is the asset that compounds.

Stage 06 · Analysis

Assessment emphasis

Five signals, weighted and normalised to a common scale: how often an objective is assessed, how recently, for how many marks, whether it is on the current specification, and how confident its extraction was. Not a frequency count wearing a formula.

Stage 07 · Capacity

Calendar capacity

A term is modelled as available teaching minutes, not as a list of dates. A school day is not a slot: assemblies, labs and swapped periods all change what a class actually gets, and the solver needs that derived number.

Weekly timetable

Each slot is expanded across every matching school day in the term.

Stage 08 · Solver

Term plan

The solver places lightweight teaching units, not finished lessons. Rescheduling a unit descriptor is cheap; rescheduling a lesson a teacher has already edited is expensive, and that expense is what makes people refuse to replan at all.

Teaching units

One per objective, priority seeded from the emphasis scores.

Stage 09 · Solver

Disruption & replan

A replan is judged by how little it disturbs, not by how clever the rearrangement is. An optimal plan that rewrites three months of term after one closed day gets rejected by the teacher every time — so churn is a constraint in the solver, not a report written afterwards.

Stage 10 · Generation

Grounded generation

Verify, then render — never generate then cite. Evidence is retrieved, claims are drafted against it, and a different model checks each claim against the spans it cited. A claim is never stored as verified because it sounded confident.
